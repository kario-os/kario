# Battery life: the screen, the notification light, needless wake-ups

> Status: **prototype ✓ (developer machine, QEMU)**. Turning the screen off, waking it with a key, the notification light's slow blink, its going out once things are seen, and calls, alarms and chargers lighting the screen pass in the selftest; on the emulator the notification light is a userspace LED that the kernel's timer trigger blinks with a 5-second period (the selftest measures the period). Power draw on real hardware has not been measured: that **waits for a Snapdragon board** (R16).

## 0. Conclusions

- The screen is the phone's biggest draw, so: with no key pressed it goes off (30 seconds by default, 15 seconds to 5 minutes in Settings), dimmed for its last 5 seconds; while it is off the renderer turns the panel off and the Shell sends no frames.
- New SMS, new messages and missed calls **do not light the screen**: when the board has a notification light it slow-blinks (lit 0.2 seconds every 5) until the user has seen them in the notification centre or in their app.
- The software itself wakes less: with the screen off the Shell's housekeeping slows from twice a second to once every 5 seconds, and frames are drawn only when something changed, so an idle phone does not wake 60 times a second.
- Which board has which light and backlight is written in the SKU's board configuration ([24](24-sku-framework.md)), and the code drives them through `hal/` ([11](11-platform-architecture.md) §"Then what is hal/").

## 1. The screen

| Situation | Screen |
|------|------|
| No key for the timeout | First dimmed to 20% (where there is a backlight device), with the keyboard backlight turned off, then off; dimming, going off, lighting up and a new brightness all fade instead of jumping ([37](37-motion.md) §1, **not tried on a phone**) |
| Keyboard backlight | Where there is one it goes on and off with the screen, at the screen's brightness (Settings › Display and brightness); dimming turns it off first. The board configuration's `keyboard_backlight` names its LED; without it the first LED named `<device>::kbd_backlight` is used (the kernel's naming convention); `hal/backlight`'s unit tests and the Shell's tests ✓, **not tried on a phone** |
| Automatic brightness | Settings › Display and brightness › Auto brightness (off by default): the backlight follows the ambient light sensor (`hal/sensor`, IIO); the brightness set is the level in an ordinary room (300 lux), a dark room scales it down to three tenths and daylight up to two and a half times, between 10% and 100%. The reading is smoothed on a logarithmic scale, faster when it gets brighter (about 3 seconds) than darker (about 7); light that stays within a quarter of what it was does not move the level, so a hand's shadow does not make it flicker. In daylight (above 1000 lux, back below 600) the keyboard backlight goes off, automatic brightness or not. The board configuration's `light_sensor` gives the sensor's IIO name; without it the first IIO device that measures illuminance is used; on machines without a sensor it is simulated (the settings page says so). Unit tests and selftest ✓, **not tried on a phone** |
| A video, the camera's viewfinder or a video call on the screen, or something ringing | Stays on |
| Any key with the screen off | Lights it; **that press only lights it** and does nothing else (a press in a pocket does not open the menu) |
| An incoming call, an alarm, a charger plugged in, a computer asking to be trusted | Lights by itself; on a charger the status bar's battery has a lightning bolt beside it and, below full, fills from its level a step at a time and starts again, and the standby screen draws a big battery and its percentage under the date, moving with it (phone-paint times it itself and draws only the battery again, [37](37-motion.md) §3; **not tried on a phone**) |
| New SMS, new messages, missed calls | With a notification light the screen stays off and the light slow-blinks; a model without one lights the screen, and the usual timeout applies. The notification still goes into the notification centre and plays its sound |

While the screen is off:

- The Shell tells the renderer to turn the panel off (the protocol's `power`, [23](23-renderer-protocol.md)): phone-paint stops the display controller's scan-out (the CRTC) and the panel driver powers the panel and its backlight down; on again, it shows the last frame received while off, and the backlight lights once the panel is on (§7).
- The Shell draws and sends no frames; the housekeeping (freezing background apps, memory, alarms, jobs, USB) runs every 5 seconds, so an alarm rings at most 5 seconds late.
- The keypad lock still follows its own setting (Settings › Keypad lock); it is a separate matter from the screen going off.

A charger plugged in (`hal/power`, `checkCharger` in `shell/screen.go`):

- **On external power or not**: what the chargers (power supplies of type Mains or USB) say with `online`; the battery's own `status` counts only on a machine where no charger reports `online`, and then only `Charging`. A fuel gauge keeps reporting `Full` at 100% after the charger is pulled out (the 6T's bq27411); `Full` used to count as plugged in, so on a full 6T pulling the charger out and plugging it in again never lit the screen ([39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.11). Fast charging is still told by `usb_type` (`[PD]`, `[PD_PPS]`, `[DCP]` and the like, or a supply of type `USB_PD`).
- **How soon it is noticed**: when the kernel says a supply changed (a power_supply uevent, which the charger's driver sends at a plug or unplug) the Shell checks at once; without uevents, with the housekeeping (every 0.5 seconds with the screen on, every 5 with it off). In suspend: the 6T's PMI8998 charger driver makes its cable interrupt (`usb-plugin`) a wake-up interrupt, so plugging in should wake the phone, and on waking the uevent has the Shell light the screen at once (**not tried on a phone**, [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §14.10).
- **The toast waits for the charger to be known**: the PMI8998 takes a few hundred milliseconds after the plug to find out what kind of charger it is, and meanwhile `usb_type` answers `EAGAIN` (`Power.Detecting`); the screen lights first, and "Charging" or "Fast charging" comes once the charger is known, after 3 seconds at most.

## 2. The notification light

| Part | How |
|------|------|
| When it blinks | The screen is off and the notification centre holds notifications the user has not seen |
| What counts as seen | Opening the notification centre (all of them), or the app coming to the foreground (its own); an app's new notifications while it is in the foreground count as seen at once |
| How it blinks | `hal/led` uses the LED class's timer trigger: `timer` written to `trigger`, `delay_on` 200 and `delay_off` 4800 (milliseconds), at maximum brightness (short and bright costs less than long and dim). Where the driver can blink by itself (a phone PMIC's LED, such as Qualcomm's LPG) the hardware blinks and the CPU does not wake; otherwise a kernel timer switches it, still with no user-space process involved |
| Multicolor lights | The board configuration's `notify_color` gives each channel's intensity (the F(x)tec Pro1 X's `rgb:status`: green) |
| Boards without a light | No `[leds]` is configured (the OnePlus N100): an unseen notification arriving with the screen off lights the screen, and the usual timeout turns it off again |
| The emulator | The kernel has uleds (`CONFIG_LEDS_USER`); when the light the board should have does not exist, the Shell makes a userspace LED of that name itself; the timer trigger works on it the same way, and the Shell reads when the kernel switches it on and off |
| The developer machine | A simulated light in memory, whose state the selftest checks |

## 3. The software wakes less

| Before | Now |
|------|------|
| On the device the Shell woke every 16 milliseconds to see whether to draw a frame | A frame is drawn 16 milliseconds after an event (a key, an app's message, a media frame, the housekeeping), and nothing wakes otherwise; during the boot animation it still draws frame by frame |
| Housekeeping every 0.5 seconds, always | 0.5 seconds with the screen on, 5 seconds with it off |
| Background apps | Already: frozen when they leave the foreground (the cgroup freezer), with pushes and jobs woken together within a budget ([21](21-push.md), [22](22-developer-api.md)) |
| Users cannot tell how much CPU an app uses in the background | Every 30 seconds the Shell reads each app's CPU (the cgroup's `cpu.stat`, or `/proc/<pid>/stat` without cgroups) and keeps a day of it, split into foreground and background. An app that is not in a call or playing music yet averages more than 5% of a core in the background over 10 minutes is named in a system notification (once a day), whose "Stop it" (the `s` key) ends it; Settings › Battery shows the charge and lists each app's background and foreground CPU over the last day, by background use (`energy` in `settings.get`) |
| With the screen off the phone stayed awake | 30 seconds after the screen goes off, with no call, no sound playing, no update installing, no computer on the USB link and no app woken for a push or a job, the whole phone suspends: to RAM where the board has it (`mem` in `/sys/power/state`), else to idle (`freeze`). Before it does, the RTC's wake alarm is set 3 seconds before the next alarm, scheduled notification (`notify.schedule`, [22](22-developer-api.md) §4.4) or job deadline, 15 minutes later at the latest (the push connection catches up on its keep-alive, due jobs run together); the baseband's calls and messages and the keys wake it any time (on the 6T the modem's GLINK channel is not a wake-up source by default; `phone-qcom-wake`'s udev rule enables it, **not tried on a phone**, [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.12). After a wake, what it brought (a push, a message) gets 10 seconds first. The kernel's `wakeup_count` is read before deciding and written back when suspending: a wake-up event in between (a key press) makes the kernel refuse, and it is not lost (`hal/pm`, `shell/sleep.go`). While someone else holds a wake lock (`/sys/power/wake_lock`, the bring-up image's `phone-bringup`) the kernel does not suspend and that read waits, so the Shell does not try and names `wake lock …` as what keeps the phone up ([39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.10). Each time what keeps it awake changes, the log gets a line `sleep: awake (…)` (leaving out what passes within seconds: the screen on or just off, just woken, something due), so a night without sleep says why; at a wake it logs `sleep: woke after …s (irq N; wake-up sources …)`, with, besides `pm_wakeup_irq`, the wake-up sources in `/sys/class/wakeup` whose `wakeup_count` rose while suspended (which tells the alarm, the modem and the charger apart). The bring-up image's `phone-bringup` is held only while a computer has the USB console configured (before 2026-11-26 it was held always, and the 6T's battery was empty after a night, [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.12; **not tried on a phone**). The Shell's clock is now the wall clock, so time asleep counts towards alarms and jobs (Go's monotonic clock stands still while suspended). Only on a phone whose board configuration says it can be woken from suspend (`suspend` in `sku.json`: real boards by default, the emulator's board not); the developer machine never suspends; nor does a board that can, where no key can wake the system (no input device's `power/wakeup` is `enabled`), since its user could not wake it |

## 6. The clock: the RTC's wake alarm and the time while the phone is off

> Status: unit tests ✓, **not tried on a phone** (what to check on the 6T: [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §14.10).

The RTC (real-time clock) is a counter in the PMIC or the SoC that keeps running while the phone is off: at boot the kernel sets the system clock from it (`CONFIG_RTC_HCTOSYS`), and in suspend its wake alarm wakes the phone (§3). Boards' RTCs differ, so the board configuration says how its RTC behaves in `[rtc]` ([24](24-sku-framework.md) §1, §2), handed to the Shell, `hal/pm` and `phone-clock` as `rtc` in `sku.json` (`hal/rtc`):

| Field | Default | Meaning |
|------|------|------|
| `read_only` | `false` | It cannot be set: `RTC_SET_TIME` fails (the OnePlus 6T's PMIC RTC on mainline answers `ENODEV`). Setting the time does not write it |
| `offset` | `false` | The system time is kept as an offset from this read-only RTC, and at boot the clock is set from the RTC plus the offset; only together with `read_only = true` |
| `alarm` | `relative` | How the wake alarm is written: `relative` is `+N` (N seconds from the RTC's own time, right on any RTC); `absolute` is the wake time itself, right only where the RTC agrees with the system clock (settable, and written when the time is set), never on a read-only RTC (on the 6T it once put the alarm 57 years ahead) |

The boards now: the OnePlus 6T has `read_only = true`, `offset = true`; the OnePlus 6 (the same PMIC) and the Redmi Note 4X (PM8953, the same driver) follow it, marked in `unverified`; QEMU's pl031 can be set and says `read_only = false`; the two Snapdragon module boards say nothing (settable by default), to be decided on the boards ([41](41-design-gaps.md) §11). An offset is also right on a settable RTC, so writing `offset` for a board one is not sure of does no harm.

**A settable RTC** (as hwclock does it): after the Shell has set the time (by hand, the network's time NITZ) it writes the RTC, and where systemd-timedated set it, timedated writes it itself (the time set by hand goes through it; NITZ only comes while automatic time is on, when NTP is on too and timedated refuses SetTime, so the Shell sets the system clock directly and writes the RTC itself); while NTP is synchronised the kernel writes it every 11 minutes (`CONFIG_RTC_SYSTOHC`); at shutdown `phone-clock save --if-synced` writes it once more while NTP is synchronised. At boot the kernel sets the clock from it.

**A read-only RTC** keeps an offset, as Android does:

- The offset is the system time less the RTC's (in milliseconds), kept in the persistent area's `/var/lib/slots/rtc-offset.json` (`offset_ms`; when it was measured, `at`; what set the time, `by`: `user`, `network`, `ntp`, `save`), which a factory reset does not erase: the clock is the phone's, not the user's data. The RTC counts whole seconds, so it is read until its second turns over, when it is that second exactly: the error is the polling interval (5 milliseconds), not a second.
- When it is measured: as soon as the Shell has set the time (by hand in Settings, the NITZ telephonyd reports); while automatic time is on and NTP is synchronised (timesyncd's `/run/systemd/timesync/synchronized`), every 5 minutes, kept anew only when it is over 0.5 seconds from the one kept (the drift between the RTC's crystal and NTP); a time set while it is being measured is measured again. At shutdown `phone-clock save --if-synced` checks it once more when the kernel says NTP is synchronised (`adjtimex`: `STA_UNSYNC` clear, an error bound under 16 seconds; setting the time by hand ends that). A time changed another way (`date -s` over SSH) is not kept: `phone-clock save` keeps it.
- At boot `phone-clock.service` (`phone-clock restore` in kario-system) runs before `sysinit.target`, `time-set.target` and systemd-timesyncd, so the services with the default dependencies (the Shell, telephonyd) start with the time set: the clock is the RTC's second plus half a second (where in that second it was read is not known) plus the offset. The RTC's driver may be a module (rtc-pm8xxx is `=m` in Nura's 6T kernel; Kario's 6T kernel has it built in, [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §8.3): when udev has loaded it and it registers, the kernel sets the clock from the RTC's count once (`CONFIG_RTC_HCTOSYS`), and a clock set before that would go back to 1970. So it comes after udev's coldplug (systemd-udev-trigger) and waits until the RTC is there and the kernel has set the clock from it (`hctosys` is 1; at most 15 seconds, and 1 second more where the kernel does not do that), then sets it. When the RTC plus the offset is before the time the offset was measured, the RTC lost its power and started over and the offset no longer holds: the clock is left alone (to timesyncd's saved time and the network) and the log says why; it is also left when it is right already (less than 1 second off).
- Without an offset (a new phone, an RTC that lost its power) the phone boots at the RTC's own count (the 6T's: 1970 and some days), systemd-timesyncd moves it to the time it saved last, until NTP or NITZ gives the time.
- The selftest's time steps (`timeshift`, the simulated suspend) move the Shell's own clock skew (`clockSkew`), not the system clock: they cannot reach the RTC or the offset.

**Whose clock it is**: only the system that owns the clock sets it (`Ours` in `hal/rtc`): root, not in a chroot (the root directory is not process 1's), and no `PHONE_CLOCK=host` in the environment. The chroot kit ([24](24-sku-framework.md) §6.2) gives every command it runs in the chroot `PHONE_CLOCK=host`: the clock there is Nura's, and the Shell sets no time, turns no NTP on or off (the image's timedated is there, but the system bus is Nura's), writes no RTC and keeps no offset, Settings shows no "Set date and time", and the log says so in one line; the kit's `setup` sets the phone's clock from the build machine's. On a flashed phone Kario owns the clock, that is its own services (`phone-clock`, the Shell on the panel); the kit testing the whole phone (`--whole`) gives every command `PHONE_CLOCK=host` too, so the Shell under test leaves it alone.

`phone-clock show` lists the board's RTC, its time (`since_epoch`), whether the kernel set the clock from it at boot, the system clock, whether NTP is synchronised, the offset kept and how far the RTC plus the offset is from the system clock. The kernel's alternatives (`allow-set-time` in the device tree, or an nvmem cell for the offset in the PMIC's SDAM) and why the 6T does not use them: [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §11.2.

## 7. The power key, the system's logind, the watchdog and power-off

> Status: the power key belonging to the Shell ✓ on QEMU (the "power key" case; in an image without the logind drop-in below, logind powers the emulator off at the press, 2026-11-24); pressing the power key again and again (the panel changed once at a time, ending as the last press left the screen) ✓ on the development machine (unit tests, selftest) and on QEMU (2026-11-25), **not tried on a phone**; the power menu (a short press of the power key closes it, with only the power and volume keys they answer it, the screen going off closes it) ✓ on the development machine (unit tests, selftest, 2026-11-25), **not tried on a phone**; device attributes not through Go's file objects and the loop's watchdog: unit tests ✓ (2026-11-25), **not tried on a phone**; the watchdog, power-off and waking with the power key are **not tried on a phone** (what to check on the 6T: [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §14.10).

- **The power key and the lid belong to the Shell**: a press of the power key turns the screen off or on, a long one opens the power menu, and when the whole phone suspends is the Shell's decision (§1, §3, `shell/keys.go`, `shell/sleep.go`). The renderer reads the keys: phone-paint's input helper opens every input device with Enter, a power key or volume keys (gpio-keys, the PMIC's power key) and gives the power key to the Shell ([23](23-renderer-protocol.md)). systemd-logind reads those devices too (udev tags every input device with keys or switches `power-switch`), and by default it powers off at the power key and suspends when a lid closes (a Hall sensor's `SW_LID`: the 6T's magnetic cover). The image's drop-in (`/usr/lib/systemd/logind.conf.d/50-phone.conf`, installed by a `systemd-conf` bbappend) sets the handling of every power, reboot, suspend and hibernate key systemd 259 knows (short and long press) and of the three lid cases to `ignore`, and `IdleAction=ignore`; the Shell needs no logind inhibitor. The chroot kit runs on Nura, whose logind it is: `shell-start` still blocks it with `systemd-inhibit`/`elogind-inhibit` ([24](24-sku-framework.md) §6.2).
- **Short and long presses**: the renderer reads the power key (phone-paint's `PowerKey`): let go before the long press time, it is a short press, reported at the release (with how many milliseconds it was down, `ms`); held until the long press time with the kernel saying it is still down, it is a long press (the power menu), and its release reports nothing more; autorepeats only mean "still down". When a release was missed (the device opened again, its event buffer overrun), the renderer asks the kernel (`EVIOCGKEY`) when the long press comes due: if the kernel has the key up, it was a short press, not the power menu; the next press starts over, so the key is never "held" for good. The long press time is Settings › Accessibility › Keys › Long press (600 ms by default, the other keys' too), never below 500 ms for the power key: the Shell sends it as `power_long` in the `keys` message ([23](23-renderer-protocol.md) §3). 600 ms stays: the same as the 9-key pad's long press and the end key's (which is the power key on a model without one); a little longer than Android's 500 ms (`config_globalActionsKeyTimeout`, the power menu on a long press), so a slowish tap is still a tap. Most current phones give the side key's hold to the voice assistant (the iPhone, recent Pixels and Samsungs by default) and the power menu to the side key held with a volume key; Kario has no assistant, so its long press is the power menu.
- **The power menu** (`openPowerMenu` in `shell/keys.go`, **not tried on a phone**): Cancel, Power off (number key 1), Restart (2), Turn airplane mode on/off (3), in that order, Cancel chosen when it opens, so OK never powers off at once. A short press of the power key while it is open:
  - **Only the power and volume keys** (none of the input devices connected can press OK, as the renderer reports in the `input` message's `ok`, [23](23-renderer-protocol.md); the OnePlus 6T without a keyboard): the volume keys move the highlight (up, down; the volume stays), and a short press of the power key chooses the entry highlighted: Cancel if it was not moved, closing the menu; volume down once to Power off, then a short press, powers off. The menu's first line says "Volume keys move · power key chooses".
  - **A keyboard or mouse that can press OK connected, or a model with a keypad** (QEMU, the 9-key and QWERTY boards, the 6T with a Bluetooth keyboard): a short press of the power key only closes the menu, whatever is highlighted, so the power key never powers off from the menu; OK or a number key chooses, Back or the end key closes; the volume keys set the volume as always. On a model without a separate power key (the end key doubling as it) a press of the end key also only closes the menu, without going back to standby (during a call it still closes the menu and hangs up).
  - Either way the press closing the menu starts no double press: the very next press turns the screen off, never opening the quick card view, and the screen stays on. The quick card view (the card menu two presses open) closes with a short press of the power key as well.
  - The screen going off for any reason (the timeout, the power key, a panel that did not come on) closes the power menu and the quick card view: they are not there when the screen comes back. A lid (the Hall sensor) does not turn the screen off now: logind ignores it and the Shell does not read it.
  - After a renderer restart the Shell counts the input devices afresh (the new renderer reports each again). Unit tests `TestPowerMenu*` (the 6T's `input` messages fed to the Shell as they come), `the_phones_buttons_cannot_press_ok` (phone-paint); the four "power menu" selftest cases (`buttons-only on` stands in for the 6T without a keyboard, `power-key off` for a board whose end key is the power key too).
  - How each kind of phone (with a power key, with the end key as the power key, with only the power and volume keys; T9 and QWERTY) opens the power menu and chooses Power off: the "How to power off" table in [18](18-nokia-form-factors.md) §5.1. Holding a key never powers off directly (decided: holding Android's key opens a menu too, and a key pressed in a pocket must not switch the phone off); the reset after a hold of ten seconds or so is the PMIC's ("Holding the power key down restarts the phone" below).
- **While an alarm rings** (`powerPressed` in `shell/keys.go`, `cardPickKey` in `shell/cards.go`, `snooze` in `shell/alarm.go`, 2026-11-26): a short press of the power key snoozes it, as on Android: the alarm sound stops, it rings again in 5 minutes, the toast says "Rings again in 5 minutes", standby shows "Alarm HH:MM (snoozed)"; the press starts no double press (the very next press turns the screen off as usual). Held, the key still opens the power menu, with the alarm ringing on beneath it; a short press of the power key closes the menu, and only the next one snoozes. On a board without a separate power key the end key is the power key, pressed and held alike; where the board has a separate power key the end key leaves the alarm alone (before, the end key left the alarm alone on every board, and holding it opened no power menu). Only OK (or tapping Stop on the touchscreen) stops the alarm, never the power or the end key. Before, a short press of the power key took the generic path: a lit screen went off and the alarm rang on (the 6T without a keyboard could neither stop nor snooze it). The volume keys are unchanged: alarms share the ringtone volume (`applyVolumes`), so while an alarm rings they set the volume of the ringing alarm; Android's Clock lets the user choose whether the volume keys set the volume, snooze or dismiss (setting the volume by default); Kario has no such setting and adds none. Unit tests `TestPowerKeySnoozesTheAlarm`, `TestEndKeyAsPowerKeySnoozesTheAlarm` (both fail without this change), selftest cases 「闹钟响时短按电源键」 (A short press of the power key while the alarm rings) and 「闹钟响时按挂断键（没有电源键的机型）」 (The end key while the alarm rings, on a board without a power key); **not tried on a phone**.
- **The screen's changes, one at a time** (`panelSync` in `shell/screen.go`): the Shell's screen is the truth, and a key press changes what the screen needs; the Shell asks the renderer for one panel change at a time, waits for its answer (the answer to `power`, with how many milliseconds it took) and 250 ms more before the next, asks only for what the screen needs last however many presses came between, and asks for nothing when the panel already is that way. Ten presses in a row thus change the panel once or twice at most and leave it as the last press left the screen; before, every press went straight to the renderer, which turned the panel on and off one queued request after the other (with a panel taking 2 s each time, 10 s more after the presses stopped). A renderer with several `power` messages queued does only what the last says and answers each; it waits (at most 0.5 s) for the page flip on its way before turning the panel off or on, and a flip's late completion event pays for that flip only (before, it was taken for the next flip's: the next frame went into the buffer still on the panel, the next flip failed with `EBUSY`, and from then on it drew in place); with the panel on again it flips once to check, and if that flip does not complete in 0.5 s its answer carries `warn` (a command-mode DSI panel that did not come up completes no flips), on which the Shell turns the panel off and on once more (once only, the backlight waiting for the second time; logged `panel: on …; turning it off and on again`; if the second time is the same it is logged and the backlight lit anyway): after a suspend on 2026-11-22 the screen stayed black until one more mode set by hand, and this does that step by itself (`TestPanelOnWithAWarningOffAndOnAgain`, **not tried on a phone**, [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.12). The backlight lights once the panel is on: nothing is written to it before the renderer answers (the 6T's backlight belongs to the panel driver; written while the panel is off or powering up, it races the panel's power sequence over DSI, and it would show the panel's old memory first); 2 s without an answer only gets a log line, the backlight still left alone (with the renderer stuck in the panel's power sequence, writing it would stick the Shell too), and the light comes with the answer, or with a renderer started again. A panel that did not come on (the renderer reports `set_crtc`'s error) is asked for once more; failing again, the Shell's screen goes off too (matching the panel), the log says so, and the next press tries again from the start. A panel that did not go off is asked for once more as well, then left until the screen wants it on. A renderer stuck in the kernel leaves the Shell answering keys: it never waits for the renderer. Unit tests `TestPanel*`, `TestBacklightWaitsForThePanel` (a fake renderer taking 0.02–2 s a change, failing on request); selftest "power key pressed again and again".
- **The log**: every power key press (`power key: short press (held 95 ms): screen off (the panel on)`), the screen going off or on (`screen off (power key)`), every panel request and answer (`panel: asking the renderer to turn it off`, `panel: off in 180 ms`) and the `sleep:` lines go to `/run/phone/shell.log`, on a whole phone to the journal as well (`journalctl -b -u phone-display -o short-precise`, kept over a forced restart); the renderer logs each press, release and decision (`phone-paint: power key down`, `phone-paint: power key up: short press (95 ms)`) and how long each panel change took (`phone-paint: panel off in 180 ms`) in `/run/phone/gui.log` and the same journal. How to capture them on a phone: [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §14.10.
- **The Shell's loop does not stall on sysfs**: `hal/` reads and writes device attributes (power supplies, backlights, the notification light, the light sensor, the vibrator, suspend) through `hal.ReadAttr` and `hal.WriteAttr`, plain system calls, not Go's file objects. Opening a file, Go puts every file epoll takes into non-blocking mode on its own poller, and epoll takes sysfs attributes; when a driver answers a read or a write with `EAGAIN` (the PMI8998 charger's `usb_type` in the few hundred milliseconds it takes to detect the charger; `/sys/power/state` when a device refuses to suspend), Go waits for the file to become "readable", which a sysfs attribute never signals, and so waits for good. On 2026-11-25 the Shell stopped like that on the 6T: the screen on, keys and the timeout doing nothing, the log ending at `screen on (key)` ([39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.11). Now `EAGAIN` returns at once like any other error.
- **The loop's watchdog** (`shell/loopwatch.go`, phone-shell on the panel): everything the Shell does runs on one loop, and one call there that does not return stops it all, which used to leave not a line in the log. Now a beat runs on the loop every second; when none has for 5 seconds, a line says where the loop's goroutine is (its state and calls as the runtime reports them, innermost first) and how many keys wait for it, for example `loop: has not turned for 5s; the loop's goroutine [IO wait] poll.(*FD).Read < … < power.Read < shell.(*Core).power < …; 2 keys from the renderer waiting; the last power key 3s ago`, and another when it turns again: `loop: turned again after 12s`. The power key is logged as it arrives, before the loop handles it (`power key: from the renderer: short press (held 95 ms)`), so a stopped loop still shows the key reached the Shell. Other keys are only counted (what is typed stays out of the log). These lines go to the journal like the ones above.
- **Holding the power key down restarts the phone** (6T, seen by the user on 2026-11-25, [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.11): this is the PMIC's own reset (the PON's KPDPWR S2 reset: held longer than the S1 plus the S2 timer, the PMIC resets), not Kario's doing (inferred): logind leaves the power key alone (the drop-in above), the renderer reports one long press per hold and nothing after it, the Shell restarts only when Restart is chosen in the power menu, and the runtime watchdog is off (the board sets only the reboot one). The bootloader sets the time: neither mainline's pm8941-pwrkey nor the device tree programs S1 or S2 (Android's downstream driver does, from its device tree). Kario leaves it: it restarts a phone that hangs, like Android's forced restart (held about 10 seconds). How long the hold takes was not measured; how to measure it: [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §14.10.
- **How the selftest presses the real power key**: the selftest's renderer is headless and reads no keys, so the `button power` step reads the kernel's input devices that have `KEY_POWER` itself (`hal/input`), asks for the press with a line `PHONE-SELFTEST-PRESS: power <n>` on its output, tells a short press from a long one by the renderer's rules (`powerHold`, Go unit test `TestPowerHold`), gives it to the Shell and waits for the release; `button-burst power <presses> <gap ms>` asks for that many presses that far apart and gives each to the Shell as it comes. On QEMU, `yocto/qemu-buttons.py`, which `yocto/qemu-selftest.sh` starts, watches the console and answers each such line with one `system_powerdown` from the QEMU monitor (a burst's line with as many, the gap apart; only the selftest's own lines count, not another line quoting one, such as the boot selftest's heartbeat quoting the run's last line every 30 seconds, `yocto/qemu-buttons-test.py`): the virt machine's power button sits on its PL061 GPIO controller, which its device tree gives gpio-keys as `KEY_POWER` (QEMU's kernel gets `CONFIG_GPIO_PL061` and `CONFIG_KEYBOARD_GPIO` for it, `phone-power-sim.cfg`), down 100 ms a press. The "power key" and "power key pressed again and again (the real power key)" cases run only where `PHONE_SELFTEST_BUTTONS` names `power` (`require-button power`; QEMU's boot selftest sets it): on standby one press turns the screen off, 5 seconds later the system is still up, the next press turns it on; ten presses 150 ms apart, then five, then one. The pixel UI's QEMU check (`make yocto-gui`, `yocto/qemu-gui-drive.py`) uses the real phone-paint on the panel: the power button held 0.9 s opens the power menu; ten presses 150 ms apart, five, one: the Shell gets each once, each a short press, and the screen and the panel end on, off, on (the `PHONE-GUI-POWER` lines).
- **The watchdog**: by default systemd uses the hardware watchdog when rebooting (`RebootWatchdogSec=10min`): PID 1 arms it as it hands over to systemd-shutdown, which pings it up to the final `reboot()` and leaves it armed. Where the driver cannot take that timeout, `WDIOC_SETTIMEOUT` fails with `EINVAL` and systemd (259 and 261 alike) disarms the watchdog: that reboot runs without one. So the watchdog is board data (`[watchdog]`, [24](24-sku-framework.md) §2), which phone-sku installs as a `system.conf` drop-in (`/usr/lib/systemd/system.conf.d/50-phone-watchdog.conf`); boards without it keep systemd's own. The Snapdragon 845's APSS watchdog (`qcom_wdt`, `qcom,kpss-wdt` on the 32764 Hz sleep clock) has 20-bit counters, at most `0xfffff / 32764` = 32 s, and the kernel refuses only longer ones (`watchdog_timeout_invalid`): the OnePlus 6T and 6 have `max_timeout = 32`, `reboot = "30s"`. A restart is a hard reset of the SoC (pm8941-pwrkey sets the PMIC's PS_HOLD reset to it before the firmware drops PS_HOLD), so the armed watchdog does not carry over into the next boot.
- **Power-off**: the 6T's kernel uses PSCI's `SYSTEM_OFF` (`sdm845.dtsi` has a `psci` node, `CONFIG_ARM_PSCI_FW=y`, `pm_power_off = psci_sys_poweroff`), and pm8941-pwrkey has set PS_HOLD to shut down first; there is no `qcom,pshold` node, so msm-poweroff is not used. A kernel that cannot power off turns `reboot()` into a halt (the CPUs stop where they are), not a restart. Powered off with a charger plugged in, the phone is powered on again by the PMIC, which sees the charger, and the bootloader writes `androidboot.mode=charger` on the command line: the phone then boots charger mode, not the system (§9).
- **What woke the phone**: `hal/pm` reads `/sys/power/pm_wakeup_irq`, which the Shell logs as "sleep: woke after … (irq N)"; the kernel has that file only with `CONFIG_PM_SLEEP_DEBUG` (`PM_DEBUG` with `PM_SLEEP`), which the 6T's kernel lacked, so the log always said "no wake reason". Every Kario kernel now has `CONFIG_PM_DEBUG=y` (`phone-power.cfg`).
- **After the power key wakes the phone**: the press that woke it reaches the renderer once it is thawed (the kernel replays the interrupt on resume, and reports a press and a release when the key is up by then); the screen is still off when the Shell gets it, so it lights the screen. Nothing else lights the screen at a wake (the key would then turn it off). The key coming first and the Shell hearing of the wake after, or the other way round, ends the same (`TestPowerKeyAroundAWake`).

## 8. The power cost of long connections (an estimate)

Each heartbeat on an app's own long connection (`net.keepalive`, [21](21-push.md) §8) takes the cellular radio from idle to the connected state (RRC setup), sends and receives a few hundred bytes, and then the network keeps the radio connected for a tail (the inactivity timer the operator sets) before letting it go idle again; a suspended phone that wakes also stays awake for 10 seconds (`awakeAfterWake`). The energy of one wake is computed like this (`push.WakeCost`, `push.LTEWake`):

E = promotion 0.26 s × 1.2 W + tail T × 0.5 W + awake 10 s × 0.1 W (over suspend), at a nominal battery voltage of 3.85 V.

These are **assumed numbers**, of the order of published LTE measurements (older LTE phones drew about 1 W in the tail; current basebands draw less), **not measured on a Kario phone**; the tail differs between operators, so it is a parameter: 5, 10 and 20 seconds. One wake: 0.275 mAh at T = 5 s, 0.455 mAh at 10 s, 0.816 mAh at 20 s.

| Heartbeat interval | Wakes a day | mAh a day (T = 5 s) | mAh a day (T = 10 s) | mAh a day (T = 20 s) | A day of a OnePlus 6T (3700 mAh), T = 10 s |
|------|------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|
| 1 minute | 1440 | 396.1 | 655.8 | 1175.3 | 17.7% |
| 5 minutes | 288 | 79.2 | 131.2 | 235.1 | 3.5% |
| 10 minutes | 144 | 39.6 | 65.6 | 117.5 | 1.8% |
| 28 minutes | 51.4 | 14.1 | 23.4 | 42.0 | 0.6% |
| System push (the app has no connection of its own) | 0 (extra) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- **System push**: the whole phone has pushd's one connection, whose learned heartbeat is at most 28 minutes ([21](21-push.md) §2), the 28-minute row above: about 51 wakes and 23.4 mAh a day (T = 10 s), shared by **all** apps; an app using push adds no wake, only the messages that really come do (data pushes at most 20 an hour per app, §3).
- **Batched wakes**: if an app's own heartbeat and the push heartbeat each woke the phone on their own, a day's wakes would be their sum: heartbeats of 1, 5, 10 and 28 minutes with the 28-minute push heartbeat make 1491, 339, 195 and 103 wakes; Kario has the heartbeats ride the push heartbeat (any wake from half an interval on can carry them), and pushd's heartbeat rides a wake the system makes for an app alone (`radio_active`, §3), together 1440, 288, 144 and 51, that is the more frequent of the two (`push.Beat`; the unit test `TestBatchedHeartbeats` simulates a day). An app whose heartbeat is no shorter than the push heartbeat costs no extra power.
- **A cap per app**: the system wakes for one app alone at most 6 times an hour, so an app's own heartbeat adds at most 144 wakes a day, about 65.6 mAh (T = 10 s); an app asking for a heartbeat every minute gets it late when nothing else wakes the phone (its server or the NAT drops it; it should use a longer interval or the system push). Thawing a frozen app for data comes on top, at most 20 times an hour, as data pushes do.
- **Visible**: the "Own connections" section of Settings › Battery lists, per app over the last day, how often the system woke for it, how many heartbeats rode other wakes, how often it was woken for data, and the charge estimated at T = 10 s above (the wakes for it alone and for data × 0.455 mAh; the ones that rode along do not count).

## 9. Not done

- Measuring power on real hardware: the current with the screen off, how much a day of standby costs (that needs a board).
- Suspend on real hardware: whether the baseband (calls, messages) and the keys are the board's wake-up sources, and whether the notification light keeps blinking in deep sleep (a PMIC light blinking on its own does, the kernel's timer trigger stops with the CPU): needs the board. The 6T's wake-up sources were listed from the kernel source; a night's drain, how soon a call or an SMS rings while asleep, and the seconds from the power key to a lit screen have not been measured ([39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §14.19, **not tried on a phone**).
- Whether plugging in a charger wakes the 6T from suspend, and how soon the screen lights then (§1 "A charger plugged in"), **not tried on a phone**; how many seconds of holding the power key reset it (§7), not measured.
- The proximity sensor (screen off against the face during a call) is **not implemented** ([11](11-platform-architecture.md)).
- A read-only RTC's offset and setting the clock at boot (§6) are **not tried on a phone**: whether the RTC keeps running across a power-off and a reboot, whether the offset is kept right, whether the time is right after a reboot without a network all have to be seen on the 6T.
- Charger mode (§9) is **not tried on a phone** (✓ on QEMU, which has no suspend): refusing to start on a low battery, suspending with the screen off and the charge current have not been seen on the 6T.
- With only the power and volume keys (the 6T without a keyboard), no system screen but the power menu and the quick card view (§7) can be answered, **not implemented**: an incoming call (the power and volume keys only silence it, it can be neither answered nor declined), an alarm (a short press of the power key snoozes it now (§7), stopping it still takes the touchscreen or a keyboard), emergency alerts and flash messages, the SIM card's PIN, the lock screen's PIN (with a PIN set the phone cannot be unlocked), the anti-theft lock, trusting a computer over USB and confirming a Bluetooth pairing, the app-not-responding dialog. Touch (being added separately) or key conventions (like the short press of the power key that snoozes a ringing alarm) could fill this in.

## 9. Charger mode (a charger starts a phone that was off)

> Status: unit tests ✓ and the generator's test ✓ on the development machine, ✓ on QEMU (`make yocto-charger-test`, 2026-11-26: the generator picked the charger's target, the charger's screen, the system not started, the screen off after 5 s, on with the power key, power-off 3 s after the simulated chargers were unplugged); no start on a low battery, the start once the level is there, "Full" and the charge current: unit tests ✓ on the development machine (a fake sysfs), ✓ on QEMU (two boots of the same check, see "Tests" below); suspend with the screen off: unit tests ✓ on the development machine (a fake sysfs and a fake suspend), QEMU not suspending like the Shell ✓, a real suspend **not tried on a phone**; all of it **not tried on a phone** (what to check on the 6T: [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §14.10 "Charger mode").

**Why**: a phone that is off is powered on by its PMIC when a charger is plugged in (a charger is one of the reasons to power on), and the bootloader decides what to start. The OnePlus 6T's ABL has `off-mode-charge:1` and `charger-screen-enabled:1`, and then puts `androidboot.mode=charger` on the command line (seen, [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.9; a normal boot has `androidboot.mode=normal`); Android starts only its charging screen for it. Kario used to ignore the parameter, so a phone powered off with the cable in, or plugged in while off, came up as the whole system: to the user, "it turned itself back on after I powered it off".

**How it is recognised**: `phone-charger-generator` (a systemd generator in `/usr/lib/systemd/system-generators/`, installed by phone-fw). systemd runs it at boot before it loads any unit; it reads `/proc/cmdline`, and when the last `androidboot.mode=` is `charger` it points `default.target` at `phone-charger.target` and `bluetooth.target` at `/dev/null` in the generators' early directory, and writes one line to the kernel log: `phone-charger-generator: androidboot.mode=charger: booting phone-charger.target`. A `systemd.unit=` on the command line still wins (for a developer who wants another target). It is not in the initramfs: the initramfs (`slot-init`) only finds the root and mounts it, and QEMU and the A/B layout each do that differently; which target to start is systemd's business, and the generator is the same on every layout and board, and can be tested directly on the development machine (`SYSTEMD_PROC_CMDLINE` stands in for `/proc/cmdline`, as for systemd's own generators).

**What starts**: `phone-charger.target` requires only basic.target (udev, journald, the mounts, the power and input drivers) plus three services, not multi-user.target, so the Shell, telephonyd, ModemManager, iwd, apps and push do not start:

| Unit | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| `phone-charger.service` | `/usr/bin/phone-charger` (`programs/chargercmd` in kario-system), restarted 2 s after a failure |
| `phone-charger-radios.service` | Before udev's coldplug, copies the blacklists in `/usr/lib/phone/charger-modprobe.d/` into `/run/modprobe.d/`: on the 6T, `phone-qcom-charger` installs `qcom_q6v5_mss` (the baseband's remote processor, whose driver boots the baseband as soon as it loads) and `ath10k_snoc` (WCN3990's Wi-Fi). With the directory empty (QEMU) it does not run |
| `phone-charger-test.service` | Runs only with `phone.charger-test=1` on the command line (the QEMU check) |

Bluetooth: the controller's device wants `bluetooth.target`, which is masked, so bluetoothd does not start; `phone-qcom-btaddr` does not run either (`ConditionKernelCommandLine=!androidboot.mode=charger`). GPS is in the baseband and stays off with it. So no radio is on.

**The screen and the keys** (`programs/chargercmd`):

- It draws with the same renderer as the panel (phone-paint, protocol `phone.frame/1`, [23](23-renderer-protocol.md)): one standby frame, whose `status.charging` draws the status bar's bolt and the big charging battery with its percentage under the date (§1, [37](37-motion.md) §3), a line under it on what the battery is doing: "Charging, 47%", "Fast charging, 47%", "Full" when the battery itself says so (`status` is `Full`; at 100% with the battery still saying `Charging` it stays "Charging, 100%": the gauge is still topping up), "Charging, 2% - too low to start" (the next items), "No battery found", "Charger unplugged: powering off"; with no level to read (no `capacity`) there is no percentage. When the battery reports `current_now`, a second line is the charge current, such as "1520 mA": its size only, not its sign (by the sysfs ABI positive is into the battery, older drivers and `test_power` have it the other way, and which way the 6T's bq27411 goes is **not tried on a phone**), read again with the supplies every second and updated while the screen is on; none once the cable is out. The hint is "Hold the power key to start". The language, time zone, 12/24-hour clock, theme and brightness follow the Shell's saved settings (`shell.LoadStandby`, as the Shell's standby screen does).
- The screen is on for 5 s, then off; a short press of the power key lights it for 5 s, and a short press while it is on darkens it. The panel and backlight follow the Shell's rules (§7): when darkening, the backlight goes off before the panel; when lighting, the backlight comes on once the renderer answers that the panel is on; one panel change at a time.
- Holding the power key for 2 s (the `keys` message's `power_long`, reported by the renderer while the key is still held): the hint becomes "Starting…" and `systemctl reboot` runs; this time the bootloader sees a reboot as the reason and starts the whole system. A failed reboot leaves the charger's screen up, and the next hold tries again. 2 s is far shorter than the PMIC's forced reset (§7 "Holding the power key down restarts the phone").
- **Too low to start** (`bootMinLevel`): below 3%, or with the gauge itself saying the battery is nearly empty (`capacity_level` is `Critical`, the bq27411's SOCF flag, about 2% by default), a hold of the power key does not reboot: the screen lights, its line says "Charging, 2% - too low to start", the hint becomes "Starting at 3%", and charging goes on; at 3% the phone reboots into the system by itself (the user has already held the key to start it, no second hold needed). Unplugged meanwhile, it still powers off 3 s later. A board without a battery (a development board on a bench supply) is never held back; one with a battery whose level cannot be read goes by `Critical` only, never kept from starting by a 0 it could not read. Why 3%: the gauge's 0% is already its cut-off voltage under load (the bq27411's Terminate Voltage), so no separate voltage check is needed; starting the whole system (the modem's boot, the panel, every service) draws more current than a computer's USB port gives, the battery makes up the difference, and a battery at 0-2% would reach its cut-off on the way and power off, and the PMIC would see the charger and boot charger mode again, over and over; 3% of the 6T's 3700 mAh is about 110 mAh, far more than the start's few tens of seconds use (inferred), and the gauge's error near 0% is a point or two as well (so not 1%). Android's charger (AOSP healthd's charger mode) likewise ignores a held power key below the device's threshold (`ro.charger.boot_min_cap`); Kario has one number, not one per board; the current and voltage drop of a start on the 6T are **not tried on a phone**, and once measured may make it a board setting.
- **Suspend with the screen off** (`programs/chargercmd/sleep.go`, with the Shell's `hal/pm` and the same decision, `shell.OpenSleep`): on a board that can suspend (the board file's `suspend`, a kernel with `mem` or `freeze`, an RTC wake alarm, a key that can wake it), the phone suspends once the screen is off, the panel is off (the renderer answered, the backlight is off), 5 s have passed since the last key and since the screen went off, 6 s since the last wake, no power-off for an unplugged charger is coming, no charger is still being identified (`usb_type` answering `EAGAIN`) and no one else holds a wake lock; the RTC wakes it every 10 minutes to read the battery again (full, or a cable pulled whose interrupt woke nothing: known within 10 minutes at the latest), every minute while a start waits for the level. The power key and a charger's plug or unplug wake it: at the start it turns on `power/wakeup` of each charger's device that can wake the system (the 6T's PMI8998 charger with its cable interrupt `usb-plugin`; left alone when already on) and logs which (`sleep: mem 5s after the screen goes off; woken by the power key, the RTC every 10m0s and the chargers …`); every power_supply change is a wake-up event, so a plug or unplug between deciding to sleep and sleeping makes the kernel call that suspend off (the `wakeup_count` handshake, §3). For 6 s after a wake it does not sleep again: the key that woke it and the plug's uevent arrive after the thaw, and the bq27xxx driver answers reads with a reading up to 5 s old by the kernel's tick, which stops in suspend. When the power key woke it, that short press lights the screen as usual. QEMU (no `suspend` in its board file) takes the Shell's path and does not suspend, logging `sleep: off (the board cannot be woken from suspend)`. A suspend that fails (not called off by a wake-up event) turns suspending off, with a log line. The status file has `sleep` (`mem`, `freeze` or `off: …`), `sleeps` (how many times it slept) and `awake` (what keeps it awake now).
- Unplugging the charger: the screen lights and says "Charger unplugged: powering off"; if it is not back within 3 s, `systemctl poweroff` (a cable that slipped does not count). The same holds when there is no charger at the start (unplugged during the boot). A board with no battery (a development board on a bench supply) never powers off for an unplugged cable.
- Every power supply change (a power_supply uevent) is read at once; without uevents the supplies are read every second; on a board that suspends, every second while awake too (it is not awake long).
- The log goes to the journal (`journalctl -b -u phone-charger`): one line for each power key press, unplug, replug, refusal to start on a low battery (`battery at 2%: too low to start; starting at 3%`), start once the level is there (`battery at 3%: starting`), reboot or power-off, and one for each suspend and wake (`sleep: mem until …`, `sleep: woke after … (irq …)`); the current state is in `/run/phone/charger` (one JSON line: the screen, the panel, whether there is a battery, the level, whether it charges, the battery's own `status`, `current_ua` (when reported), the line of text, `too_low`, `pending` (held, waiting for the level), rebooting or powering off, `sleep`, `sleeps`, `awake`), readable over the USB console (the bring-up image's `phone-bringup-usb` is in sysinit.target and starts all the same).
- **Tests**: `programs/chargercmd`'s state machine has a test for each rule (a low battery starting once charged, `Critical`, a level it cannot read, no battery, unplugged while waiting, when to suspend, "Full", the current); `sleep_test.go` tests the handshake with a fake suspend (a key after the count wins), no sleep with the panel not off or a wake lock held, a suspend called off and one that cannot happen; `main_test.go` runs the whole program on a fake sysfs (the 6T's bq27411 and PMI8998, an RTC, `/sys/power`, a key that wakes): a hold at 2% waits and the start comes at 3%, "Full" and the current, with `suspend` in the board file `mem` written to `/sys/power/state` 5 s after the screen goes off and the charger's `power/wakeup` turned on, without it the Shell's no sleep. Without this change they fail. `make yocto-charger-test` boots twice: the first boot, after the earlier checks, sees QEMU not suspending (`sleep` is `off: …`), the screen saying "Full" and the current with `test_power` set to `full` and 1500000 µA, the line at 2% and no reboot after a hold of the power key (the emulated power button held 2.4 s), then the unplug and the power-off; the second (`phone.charger-test=start`, QEMU with `-no-reboot`) holds at 2%, sets 3%, and the phone reboots by itself.

**The other way, `fastboot oem off-mode-charge 0`**, is not used. Per AOSP's rule for fastboot ([Off-mode charging](https://source.android.com/docs/core/architecture/bootloader/fastbootd#off-mode_charging)), with it off the bootloader bypasses charger mode and boots "as if the user had pressed the power button": powering on is what the PMIC does when it sees a charger, and ABL only decides what to start, so with it off the phone still comes on, only as the whole system and without `androidboot.mode=charger` on the command line, so Kario could not even tell. That is what "powered off, on again 2 s later" on Nura on 2026-11-23 looked like. It is also a step typed in fastboot on each phone, which can be lost on another phone or after flashing OxygenOS back and Kario again, and whether the 6T's ABL takes the command at all has not been tried (`oem help` says `unknown command`, [39](39-oneplus-6t.md) §16.3). Charger mode needs no change to the bootloader's settings, shows the battery while the phone charges off, and powers off when unplugged.

**Not done**:

- The big battery is still drawn where the standby screen has it, only its lines changed; there is no charging screen design of its own (a charging animation, a big battery whose colour follows the level).
- The threshold below which it refuses to start is one number (3%), not a board setting; how much a start takes on the 6T is **not tried on a phone**.
- Suspend exists only on boards like the 6T and is **not tried on a phone**: whether a charger's plug or unplug wakes the phone from it, how fast it wakes, whether the bq27411's level after a wake is fresh, and the sign of `current_now` all have to be seen on the 6T.
- Only the 6T's bootloader says this; other boards' bootloaders (the Snapdragon module boards, QEMU) write no `androidboot.mode`, and the generator does nothing.
